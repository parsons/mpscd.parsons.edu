Rendered Realities is a responsive website that curates and explores a collection of 3D digital artworks sourced entirely from Are.na. Designed and developed from scratch using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, the site functions as both an archive and a spatial interface, showcasing how digital form and dimensionality can intersect within a browser-based environment. The project began with a thematic interest in 3D design as both a visual language and a conceptual lens. Using Are.na’s API, I gathered over thirty pieces of media—ranging from rendered sculptures and virtual environments to procedural animations and speculative objects.

These works, created by different artists and studios, are unified by their ability to simulate, distort, or reflect reality through digital space. The organizing principle of the site emerges through a grid-based interface that emphasizes material, scale, and interactivity. Users can hover, filter, and explore connections between works that might otherwise exist in isolation. The site itself becomes a kind of rendered space—inviting users to consider the tension between representation and reality, and the role interface plays in shaping meaning.

Built as part of a course exploring the integration of media, content management systems, and custom web development, this project reflects my interest in coding as a design practice. Rendered Realities is not just a website—it’s a proposition for how collections of digital culture can be recontextualized through intentional systems of navigation, structure, and interface.

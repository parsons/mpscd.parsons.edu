It all started with a dinner plan. My friend, a wheelchair user, hesitated when I suggested a new restaurant. “What if there’s no ramp? What if the tables are too close together?” At the same time, my 67-year-old mother struggles with blurry vision and small text on apps. Something as simple as booking a table shouldn’t be stressful, yet for millions of seniors and people with disabilities, it often is.

In the U.S., 57 million people live with disabilities, yet 90% of websites remain inaccessible. Many avoid dining out due to uncertainty about seating, accessibility, and navigation. Through interviews, I heard their concerns, small text is hard to read, elevators and restrooms need to be nearby, and the wrong seating can cause discomfort.

With these insights, I created Table for All, a mobile app that simplifies restaurant reservations for seniors and wheelchair users. It offers adjustable font sizes, larger buttons, text-to-speech functionality, and a table selection tool with a restaurant map highlighting entrances, elevators, and restrooms. Users can also see real images of the space and filter seating options based on their needs.

By reducing uncertainty and improving accessibility, Table for All turns dining out from a challenge into a welcoming experience. More than just an app, it’s a step toward making restaurants truly inclusive for everyone.

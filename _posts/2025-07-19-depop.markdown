Depop, a second-hand shopping app, primarily supports two user flows: buying and selling. Over five weeks, I designed a new feature to enhance buyer customization and personalization. Through research—including UI analysis, user reviews, and three user interviews with Depop’s target audience (18-26-year-olds)—I identified a need for improved trust and customization.

Brainstorming with fellow designers led to three potential features: profile customization, an outfit generator, and search filter edits. I combined the Style Finder Quiz and Outfit Generator into a streamlined solution. The Style Finder Quiz, a five-step onboarding process, tailors shopping experiences based on users’ fashion preferences. The Outfit Generator then curates looks, linking users to relevant product listings. Additional features included editable filters, a shopping cart, and pinpointed posts that display real product listings within generated outfits.

User testing revealed key insights, such as the need for a faster generator experience and the option to skip the quiz. After refining the prototype and integrating feedback, I finalized the design in Figma, ensuring alignment with Depop’s brand mission of making fashion more circular.

Future improvements include further exploring buyer-seller trust, refining edge cases, and enhancing inclusivity in the generator feature. If revisited, I’d conduct additional low-fidelity explorations, introduce animated elements, and incorporate seller perspectives. This project underscores the value of personalization in secondhand shopping, bridging the gap between user preferences and seamless discovery.
